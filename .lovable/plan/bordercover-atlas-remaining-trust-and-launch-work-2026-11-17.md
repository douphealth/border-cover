# BorderCover Atlas: remaining trust and launch work

## Goal
Make the existing checker a traceable, uncertainty-first trip-research tool—not a larger directory or a promise of personal insurance cover. Preserve the current premium visual direction and deterministic calculations.

This plan compares the uploaded audit with current source files and recorded verification. It is not a new visual, accessibility, security or hosted-permissions certification. Planning does not change the app or publish it.

## What is already present
- One pure engine shared by the checker, atlas and insurer pages; separate liability, own-car, breakdown and document rows.
- Up to five countries, previous usage including unknowns, inclusive dates, renewal warnings, fragment sharing and printing.
- Structured AA allowance/Irish-territory observations, correction reports, protected editorial tools and immutable approved revisions.
- A two-column desktop checker, stacked phone layout, expandable evidence, preview noindex and an eligible-only sitemap generator.
- Recorded verification: 29 tests and representative public-flow checks. Authenticated editorial approval and recovery drills remain unverified.

Do not rebuild these features. Extend their weakest links.

## Confirmed remaining gaps
1. **Generic results need a clearer boundary.** Generic UK profiles can produce a government legal-minimum outcome. That is not an insurer-specific verdict, but the presentation can blur the distinction. Other-market fallbacks are already uncertain.
2. **Calculation wording is too definitive.** “Remaining after this trip” lacks adjacent policy-year assumptions. The interface hides the numeric renewal-crossing result, but the engine still returns one.
3. **Evidence is not connected to every material claim.** Most displays are source-level; the structured AA passages cover allowances and Ireland, not every benefit. Approved Cloud revisions are displayed separately rather than serving as the published rule authority.
4. **Maintenance signals are incomplete.** Source monitoring records observations but does not create assigned review tasks. Public results do not integrate overdue, unavailable or conflicting-evidence states.
5. **Approval does not require a second person.** Reviewer checks exist, but the current approval path does not prevent the draft author approving their own revision.
6. **Reproducibility is incomplete.** A version label and current-input share link do not preserve historical evidence or pin a future replay to its original rule release.
7. **Research depth is not shown before market selection.** Inventory totals are accurate but do not explain which combinations have claim-level reviewed evidence.
8. **Owner accountability, hosted access checks, abuse protection and live operations remain launch prerequisites.** Documentation is not proof that these have been completed.

## Phase 1 — remove misleading reassurance

### Generic profiles and research scope
- Present generic selections as **“Insurer-specific terms not researched.”** Never infer equivalent cover, a personal allowance or an insurer-specific exclusion from them.
- Keep statutory minimums and border guidance in a separately labelled official-guidance section with their own citations; they are not personal-policy approval.
- Add “My insurer isn’t listed” requests to the protected editorial backlog, using minimal information and the same abuse controls as corrections.
- Show research depth beside market choices: approved/current claim coverage, partial research and unavailable evidence. Derive this from provenance, not profile counts or the old `verifiedDestinations` label.
- Move inventory totals to the research register with explicit denominators. Do not claim any market is fully researched before all required material claims are approved.

### Result clarity
- Order results: research outcome and personal-cover disclaimer → trip summary → benefit rows → required actions → claim evidence → calculation details.
- Show insurer, tier, route, entered dates and known/unknown policy edition in the summary.
- Rename the arithmetic **“Calculated unused published allowance.”** Place published cap, allowance basis, entered previous use and trip usage beside it.
- Suppress definitive unused-day values throughout the result contract when previous usage is unknown or renewal counting is unresolved. Explain that the current-policy-year basis and matching policy edition must be confirmed.
- Preserve the distinction between exhausted enhanced cover and legal-minimum liability; never turn exceeding an allowance into a blanket uninsured verdict.
- Replace “Recorded” and “legacy dataset release” jargon with separate document edition, effective date, access date, human review and public research-update history. Missing dates remain explicitly unknown.

**Acceptance:** generic selections stay visibly unresearched across all surfaces; the default AA arithmetic remains 30 − 0 − 14 = 16 with assumptions; unknown usage and renewal crossings return no definitive unused allowance.

## Phase 2 — make each conclusion inspectable
- Extend the existing versioned result contract, not a parallel evaluator. Bind each material claim to insurer/product/tier, destination scope, edition, applicability, benefit, allowance basis, exceptions and exact supporting passages.
- Add per-claim evidence links next to the conclusion they support, including section/page, reviewer, access/review dates and unresolved conditions.
- Validate evidence references, nonempty passages, unique IDs, applicable dates and required fields. Do not make imported observations appear approved.
- Introduce explicit current/overdue/unavailable/conflicting research states. Preserve distinctions: an unreachable source is not automatically an exclusion; a material conflict cannot yield an affirmative research outcome.
- Carry source-change observations into deduplicated, assigned review tasks. Automation can propose work but cannot change published rules or reset human review dates.
- Require an authorized second person to approve consequential changes, with author and approver identities and an immutable record. Verify the rule both server-side and in hosted access controls.
- Resolve the current split between Cloud approvals and bundled eligibility: generate a reviewed, validated release artifact containing the approved claim provenance and rule dataset. All public surfaces and indexing decisions consume that same artifact.
- Archive immutable rule/evidence releases. Shared research links carry a release identifier; never silently replay an old link against changed rules. Unavailable historical releases show a clear warning rather than a recreated answer.

**Acceptance:** each affirmative material claim has approved applicable provenance; self-approval fails; changed/overdue/conflicted claims require appropriate review; historical links reproduce the original release or explicitly report that it is unavailable.

## Phase 3 — refine the desktop and phone journey
- Retain the existing desktop trip builder/result layout; improve summary hierarchy without a decorative redesign or more directory pages.
- On phones, provide **Policy → Journey → Research result**, preserving inputs, a compact persistent summary and an obvious Edit action. Keep unresolved conditions visible outside collapsed evidence.
- Add accessible result-update announcements without announcing every slider movement; focus the outcome after a deliberate check action.
- Keep full country names, explicit field labels, keyboard navigation, validation and predictable dimensions.
- Extend tailored confirmation checklists and printable summaries to include claim evidence, applicable release and research dates. Explain that trip sharing exposes the selections to anyone with the link.
- Keep sharing fragment-based and unpersisted; do not add stored-trip accounts or pretend local links are deletable remotely.

**Acceptance:** complete the same real-input journey on desktop and phone; edit and return without losing inputs; inspect uncertainty, evidence, sharing and print output; test keyboard, screen reader, zoom and reduced motion. Target WCAG 2.2 AA without claiming certification.

## Phase 4 — prove security and operations
- Test signed-out, ordinary signed-in, reviewer and independent approver access against hosted Cloud. Inspect direct data access as well as interface restrictions; public approved projections must not expose private author IDs or draft/report contents.
- Add enforceable abuse protection to public report/request submission, with bounded input and useful failure states.
- Expand tests: allowance −1/exact/+1, unsupported tier, all six generic fallbacks, unknown usage, renewal safeguards, mixed transit rules, missing/conflicting/overdue evidence, empty provenance, duplicate IDs, denied self-approval and historical replay.
- Run existing regression tests, public journey checks, dependency/security scans and route metadata checks after the relevant changes.
- Once published, configure uptime/error alerts and source-check scheduling; prove successful execution and alert delivery.
- Complete and document a known-good app rollback and an isolated Cloud data restore, including private-access and evidence-history checks. Define alert owner, backup custodian and retention first.
- Add minimal journey-completion, unresolved-result, source-visit, correction and stale-rule measures only after collection ownership and retention are agreed. Exclude trip dates, identities, report bodies and full share URLs.

## Phase 5 — narrow, accountable launch
- Publish real operator/contact information, editorial responsibility, reviewer credentials, corrections procedure, retention and actual funding/affiliate disclosure. Do not invent these facts.
- Start with **AA UK → France and Ireland as the proposed review scope**, not as already launch-ready: the current observations support some key claims but complete accountable benefit/document review is still needed.
- Keep other combinations explicitly research previews. Expand only when maintenance capacity and approved evidence support them.
- Connect and verify the intended GitHub account/repository, choose the production domain and obtain qualified legal review.
- Keep previews noindex. Enable production indexing only for substantive pages whose complete material-claim provenance is approved, current and conflict-free; empty observation lists must fail eligibility. Add production canonicals and generate the eligible sitemap from the same release.

## Owner inputs and external prerequisites
Implementation of the unblocked improvements can proceed first. These items cannot be fabricated or certified from source code:
- Operator identity, public contact, funding/affiliate status and retention decisions.
- Actual reviewer and independent approver, with signed-up accounts and owner-authorized permissions.
- Qualified legal assessment.
- GitHub connection/repository confirmation and production origin/domain.
- Alert recipient, monitoring arrangement and isolated restore environment.

## Technical approach
- Preserve TanStack Start and the original pure engine. Extend one versioned result/provenance contract for checker, atlas, insurer pages, sharing and print.
- Keep Cloud drafts/history separate from published releases; use server-validated roles, least-privilege projections and migration-backed authorization tests.
- Use the existing semantic tokens and design components. No new live AI coverage decisions, confidence percentages, bulk directory generation or alternative rule engines.
- Record structural decisions in AGENTS.md and progress in roadmap.md during implementation. Update operations documentation with observed outcomes, never promised verification.

## Delivery order
1. Generic boundaries, calculation precision, research-depth presentation and public date labels.
2. Claim linkage, independent approval, release reproducibility and review-task lifecycle.
3. Mobile stages, accessible result hierarchy and revised exports.
4. Hosted security verification, expanded tests, abuse controls and operations drills.
5. Owner-approved narrow launch; gradual evidence-backed expansion.
